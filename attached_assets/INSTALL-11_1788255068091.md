# Patch 11 — shot size as a planned field

Applies to `feat/prompt-kit-script-variants`. Patches 8, 9 and 10 are already
on the branch (I verified the code, not just the commit messages — Replit
committed them under different subjects).

```bash
git am 0011-*.patch
```

No schema migration. `shotSize` lives inside the existing `storyboard` JSONB
column, so only the TypeScript type changed. No new dependencies.

## The defect

Every shot in your last character render was an extreme close-up, even though
the planner prompt asks to "vary the coverage by mixing wide establishing
frames, medium shots, intimate close-ups". Two structural reasons:

- As prose it was one clause among six competing demands in the same sentence —
  camera movement, light quality, tactile detail, a non-repetition rule, an
  instruction not to name the character. Shot scale is what lost.
- `sceneKeyframePrompt` then said only "cinematic composition", while its own
  identity language ("place the exact character from the reference") pulls hard
  toward a face-forward frame, because the reference *is* a portrait.

## The change

`shotSize` is now an enumerated field the planner returns per scene. It is
clamped the way the costume lock is, persisted on the storyboard so an approved
plan renders the coverage it was reviewed with, and stated to the image model in
camera terms:

| size | what the image model is told |
|---|---|
| wide | character full-length inside the location, the place clearly readable |
| medium | framed from about the waist up, enough setting behind them |
| close | head and shoulders, face occupying much of the frame |

## The constraint that makes it principled

**A speaking scene can never be wide.** That is not a style rule — a wide shot
puts the face at a fraction of frame height and the lip-sync model crops around
it. It is the same face-pixel constraint that governs the upload path, arriving
here as a composition rule.

Enforced in three places: the prompt offers only medium and close on a synced
job; the clamp rewrites a wide it is handed anyway (hand-edited or replayed
plans); and `normalizeShotSize` rescues a board approved before lip sync was
switched on.

## Two deliberate choices

**The field is required, not optional.** Making it required meant the
typechecker named every site that builds a plan — including two storyboard
resume paths that would otherwise have silently defaulted and changed a video's
shape after approval. Optional would have compiled and shipped that bug.

**A missing value falls back to a rotation, not one size.** Even a model that
ignores the field produces something that cuts.

## Verified

Applied with `git am` to a fresh clone of the branch; api-server typecheck
clean; videoGen suite **799 passed**, against the same two pre-existing failures
documented in INSTALL-8-9.md.

One existing test pinned the word "coverage" in the prompt and now pins the
enumerated field instead. Worth knowing that I changed it: the mechanism
changed, and the old assertion was checking for the thing that did not work.

## What to look at

Render one character story and just watch it. You should see the framing change
between shots instead of eight identical close-ups. If lip sync is on you will
see medium and close alternating and no wides — that is correct, not a bug.

Wides become available the moment a scene exists that nobody speaks over, which
is the reaction-shot work.
